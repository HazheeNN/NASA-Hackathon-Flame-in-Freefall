# UI/UX polish plan: the Flame Lab first, then every panel

**Goal:** every screen a judge sees looks designed rather than assembled, works at 360 px, tablet, 1440 px and on a projector, and gets the honesty story across in about 5 seconds. **The Flame Lab (`/`) is the product.** It gets perfected first, and the other panels follow in the order below.

**Written:** 14 Nov 2026, from the code on `main` at `072131c` and screenshots of every page at 1440 × 900 and 360 × 780 (taken in a session and not committed). **Status: plan only, nothing built yet.** Each work package (WP) below can be done by one teammate's Claude Code session from this file alone.

## Quick start: one teammate, one Claude session (start here)

One teammate does every work package with one Claude Code session (the builder). There is no separate critic session (removed 15 Nov): the teammate reviews each package on its Vercel preview.

**Order (Flame Lab first, it's the core):** WP-L1 (includes the shared pieces of WP-F1) → L2 → L3 → L4 → L5 → L6 → L7 → L8 → P0 → P1 → P2 → P3 → P4 → P5 → P6. **If time runs short,** stop after L8 + P1, since that's what judges see. Cut P5 → P4 → P3 first.

### The loop, for every work package

1. The builder takes the next unticked package in `docs/status.md` section 3, creates the branch `ui/<package-id>`, builds it exactly as written, checks its own screenshots at 360 px, tablet, 1440 px and 1920 px (scratchpad only, never shared or committed), and runs `npm run check` and `npm run data:validate`.
2. It opens a PR titled `UI: <package-id> <name>` and gives the teammate the Vercel preview link and a short summary in words.
3. The teammate looks at the preview. If they don't like it, the builder changes it or tries another approach on the same branch.
4. On the teammate's OK and green CI, the builder merges (squash) and starts the next package.

const { chromium } = require('playwright');
(async () => {
  const b = await chromium.launch({ args: ['--use-gl=angle', '--use-angle=swiftshader', '--enable-unsafe-swiftshader'] });

## 1. What the judges score, and what that means for the UI

From the Space Apps *Judging & Awards* rules (5 criteria, equal weight). Deadline: **Sun 15 Nov, 11:59 pm local**. The submission has a **30-second video** and up to **7 slides**, so the first 30 seconds of the Flame Lab *is* the pitch.

| Criterion | What judges look for | What the UI must do |
|---|---|---|
| **Impact** | Does it help crews and mission planners? | Every lab state ends in a **plain verdict** ("NASA has tested this", "never tested at Moon gravity") and links to the ranked insights for that world. |
| **Creativity** | Is it new? | The live flame that changes with gravity and oxygen, the **Earth vs selected-world comparison**, and the red/blue tested map are our signature. Make them unmistakable. |
| **Validity** (science + *usability*) | Accurate and usable? | Citations on every number, "illustration" labels, honest red. Usable means no clipped text, a keyboard path through everything, readable on a projector, and nothing that looks broken. |
| **Relevance** | Uses NASA data, answers the challenge? | NASA sources visible on screen (FLEX Table IX, finding IDs) without a click, plus the "summarize, rank, interpret" story: Findings → Insights → Ask. |
| **Presentation** | Clear, polished, tells a story? | The first 900 px of `/` tells the whole story: question, flame, controls, verdict. One consistent look on every page. |

**North star for the 30-second video:** open `/` → drag oxygen from 21 to 34 % (the flame grows) → click **Moon** (the flame turns into a teardrop and the panel turns red: "Never tested at 0.165 g") → click **Compare with Earth** → cut to Insights. Every WP below serves that path.

## 2. What the screenshots show today (the problems to fix)

Flame Lab at 1440 × 900:

1. **The instrument doesn't fit above the fold.** The header block takes ~380 px, so the stage starts at about y 510 and the flame card ends below the fold. A judge's first screen is a headline and the top of three boxes.
2. **The flame reads as a small blue "donut".** The ring is about 20 % of the canvas width, inside a ~500 px dark box, with no inner glow. It looks like a loading spinner. Real FLEX images show a soft, glowing blue sphere, and with heptane a faint orange-yellow soot shell inside it.
3. **The three columns end at different heights.** The controls column runs ~350 px past its content, the centre card ends ~360 px above the right column, and the right column stacks 3 cards. The page looks unfinished.
4. **The world picker is two screens down** ("Choose a world" sits below everything), yet switching worlds is the most dramatic thing the lab does. Clicking a world scrolls the page back up (`scrollIntoView`), which feels jumpy.
5. **The verdict is buried.** "What NASA has measured" is four rows of small text. The answer to "has anyone tested this?" should be one big line with a colour and a word.
6. **Values jump.** Moving a slider snaps the flame instantly. Without easing it feels like swapping pictures, not watching a flame.
7. **A bug: presets outside the slider ranges.** `apollo-1-pad` is 100 % O₂ and `apollo-pad-60-40` is 60 %, but `O2_RANGE` is 10–45 (`flame-lab.tsx:24`). The slider pins at 45 while the label says 100. The same risk applies to pressure (20–320 kPa; fine today).
8. **Phone (360 px):** the headline and lead fill the first screen. The flame starts at y 460 and the sliders are 3 screens below it, so you can't watch the flame while you drag. The "Illustration…" caption overlaps the flame card's bottom edge.
9. Small issues: the fuel buttons wrap onto two lines in a 300 px column, "Cabin air on International Space Station" wraps to two lines, the fixed `pr-36` on the stage header wastes space, and lab state isn't in the URL (it can't be shared or deep-linked for the video).

Other panels:

10. **Gaps map:** marker labels collide ("ISS cabin air" and "Earth sea level" print on top of each other near 21 % / 101 kPa), and the map is taller than the first screen. Otherwise strong.
11. **Insights:** a good grid, but every card has the same weight. The #1 card should feel like #1. Card heights jump because text lengths differ.
12. **All pages:** page headers are big (52 px plus a long lead) and push content below the fold. Detail pages have no breadcrumbs or back links, and the nav has 7 items with no grouping.

### WP-L2: The flame itself (~120 min; the biggest visual win)

File: `src/components/flame-canvas.tsx`. The rule (DESIGN.md › Don'ts) stays: **it's an illustration and computes no numbers.** Every visual trend must match a finding we cite (list below). Don't add any effect we can't cite.

| Visual trend | Cite (already verified in `data/findings.json`) |
|---|---|
| Spherical in microgravity | F-037 "Without gravity a droplet flame stays spherical" |
| Visible flame smaller than the full flame (faint outer zone) | F-035 |
| Weak, dim flames possible in microgravity; burns at lower O₂ | F-026, F-033, F-001 |
| Teardrop + flicker under gravity; a flame makes its own ~30 cm/s wind on Earth | F-027 |
| Airflow pushes and stretches the flame; flames react fast to airflow | F-015, F-006 |

Changes:

1. **Size:** the flame diameter should be **45–55 % of the canvas's short side** at ISS 21 %. Today the ray is `rd = normalize(vec3(uv * 1.15, 1.6))` with `R = 0.22…`. Zoom the camera to `uv * 0.72` (try 0.65–0.8). Keep the bounding sphere big enough (recompute `cc = … - 1.25`) so the teardrop at 1 g and 40 % O₂ isn't clipped. Check that the top of the flame stays inside the canvas at Earth, 45 % O₂, 320 kPa.
2. **No more donut:** a microgravity flame should look like a glowing sphere.
   - Make the shell thicker and softer in microgravity: `w = 0.045 + 0.02*buoy + 0.008*pn` (today 0.02).
   - Add an **inner volume glow**, a low-density blue emission inside the shell (`inner * 0.18 * (1.0-buoy)`), so the face of the sphere isn't black.
   - Add an **outer faint zone** (F-035): a second, wider, very dim shell at `R*1.35`, alpha ≤ 0.15.
   - For **heptane in microgravity**, add a thin **orange soot shell inside the blue one** at `R*0.7`, intensity `uSoot*(1.0-buoy)*0.35*o2n`. That's what FLEX imagery shows. Methanol (`soot 0.18`) stays almost pure blue.
   - Add a slow "breathing" wobble in microgravity: fbm amplitude 0.015, time scale 0.2. It should feel alive but calm.
3. **Ease every parameter:** keep a `current` copy of the uniforms in the render loop and move it toward the target each frame with `cur += (target - cur) * (1 - exp(-dt/0.25))` (a ~250 ms time constant). Gravity eases over 600 ms to match the world backdrop crossfade. With reduced motion, skip the easing and draw once.
4. **Bloom without a second pass:** after tone mapping, add `col += haloCol * pow(halo, 0.6) * 0.25`. Raise the halo strength with O₂ so 34 % visibly glows more than 21 %.
5. **Droplet:** keep it, but make it `dr = 0.035` and slightly reflective. It anchors the scale.
6. **Projector safety:** keep the pixel cap (900 k). Measure FPS at 1920 × 1080 in Chrome. If it's under 50, lower `STEPS` to 48 when `el.width*el.height > 700k`.
7. **Fallback** (`failed`): replace the CSS ring with a static PNG, `public/flame-fallback.png`, rendered from the shader at ISS 21 %. Credit it as "Illustration". Don't use a NASA photo here.

**Check:** take 7 screenshots of the stage only: ISS 21 %, ISS 34 %, ISS 15 % (dimmer, smaller), Moon 34 %, Mars 34 %, Earth 21 % heptane, Earth 21 % methanol. Someone who hasn't seen the app should be able to tell all seven apart, and none should look like a ring or a spinner. FPS at 1920 × 1080 is ≥ 50 (Chrome DevTools › Rendering › FPS meter), and reduced motion draws one still frame.

### WP-L8: Ignite button, watch it light and burn out (~90 min, after L2 + L4)

| Part | Where it comes from | Shown as |
|---|---|---|
